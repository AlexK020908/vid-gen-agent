Write each visual prompt as one short paragraph, 20 to 40 words, describing a single frame:

- Who is in it and what they're doing, at the moment the narration describes. The main character is always "the stick figure", and cast members go by their cast names ("Mrs. Okafor"). The pipeline gives the artist a reference picture of each of them, so don't describe their bodies, faces or clothes; what they carry, put on or have wrong with them (a backpack, body armor, a bandaged hand) is fine. Anyone else (the infected, a crowd, a passer-by) gets a short description: how many, how close, what they're doing.
- Where: the scene's place is drawn from its own reference picture, so don't describe its layout again. Say which part of it the camera sees and from where (at the window, by the barricaded front door, looking down the hallway), the time of day and the weather. When the character arrives somewhere, show enough of the place that the viewer knows where they are.
- Framing: the scene's `shot`, said first ("Wide shot: ...", "Close-up: ..."), and the prompt shows what that framing can: the whole place and where everyone is in a wide shot, the character and what they're doing in a medium shot, one face, hand or object in a close-up.
- The mood in a word or two (tense, calm, bleak, hopeful), and the light when it matters (firelight, dusk, a flashlight beam).

The pipeline adds the channel's art style ({style_prompt}), so don't describe line work, colors or style. No words, signs, numbers or labels in the picture. When the line has a threat (zombies, animals, a storm, fire), put it in the frame where a viewer spots it at a glance: how many, how close, what they're doing. Never gory. One frame, one moment: no sequences, split panels or speech bubbles. For a clip scene, also say in a few words what moves.

You measure pictures for a cutout-animation pipeline that draws stick-figure people into painted places. Positions are percentages of the picture: x from 0 at the left edge to 100 at the right, y from 0 at the top to 100 at the bottom. Every picture you get has a red grid drawn over it with its lines labelled every 10, so read positions off the grid and be exact: a figure's feet go precisely where you say, and a figure drawn standing in the wrong place, floating above the floor or the wrong size for its depth looks absurd.

Image 1 is "{name}", a place in the cartoon, seen from the fixed camera every scene there uses. Stick-figure people will be drawn standing in it, and the pipeline needs to know where they can stand and how big they are there.

Give `seen` first: one sentence saying what the picture shows and where the camera is.

Then `floor`: the walkable floor as 3 to 6 points from the nearest place anyone stands to the farthest (a person's size is read off them by how far down the picture the feet are, so they should cover every spot's depth), in the order a person would walk them, each with x, y (where the feet go) and `height`: how tall a standing adult would be at that point, as a percentage of the picture's height (nearer the camera is taller; judge it against the doors and furniture there). Points on a different level (a step up, a stair) belong to the floor only if people walk them.

Then `spots`: the places a person would stand at or things they would use, each named in a few lowercase words joined with underscores (door_left, stairs_top, window, table), with the x, y where feet go to be there and `what` it is in a few words. Include every door, the top and the bottom of every stairway that's in the picture, every window, and the main furniture. A spot is on ground a person can stand on: never in water, on a tabletop, on a roof or in the air (the pool's spot is on its edge, the table's beside it). Where the picture shows ground on the far side of a gate, fence, railing or low wall (the road outside the gate, the yard beyond the fence, the slope below the back wall), add a spot out there too, named for it (outside_gate, beyond_fence, beyond_wall), on that ground: people who come from outside stand there, seen through the bars or over the wall.

Then `occluders`: things nearer the camera than the floor behind them, which a person standing behind would be partly hidden by, each with a `name`, a `polygon` of 4 to 8 points around it, `base_y`: the y where it meets the floor, and `see_through`. Solid ones (a table, a counter, a car, a sofa's back, a wall's near edge, a low garden wall, an open door panel) have `see_through` false: the whole polygon is drawn over whoever is behind it, so no spot lies behind one taller than a person there (a person "at the door" stands in the doorway or beside an open door panel, never behind the panel); behind a low wall is fine, as their head and shoulders show over it. Bars with gaps (a gate, a fence, a railing, a banister) have `see_through` true: only their dark bars are drawn over whoever is behind, who shows through the gaps, so a spot beyond one is fine. Draw a gate's polygon around the gate itself, from its top to where it meets the ground. Leave the list empty when nothing in the picture is like that.

Then `light`: the x, y of the main light source and `warm` true for a lamp, candle or fire, false for daylight or moonlight.

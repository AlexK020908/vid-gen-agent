# Survival

On Nov 12, 2026 the owner added a screen recording of a Reel by another creator (theforgottenarchives, not the reference): the true story of four young people who drowned in a flooded cave near Provo, Utah, in 2005. Asked whether they wanted this kind made, how long, and how real deaths should be handled, they said: "i want them made yea, long but not in parts, the video should cover everything, yea for real deaths, we wanna document it, and still show dead bodies in animated form like those videos".

**So three things differ here from `all.md`, by the owner's word:** a video is long, and tells the whole story in one, never in parts; it documents real deaths; and the dead are shown, as that Reel shows them, in animated form.

An idea here is a true story of people in mortal danger in a place that can be drawn as a cut through it: a cave, a mine, a wreck, a mountain, a sinking ship. The viewer is told the end first, then watches every small choice that led there.

## The Reel the owner showed, taken apart

It runs 2 minutes 41 seconds, about 420 words, and ends on a cliff-hanger for a second part. It had 95.7K likes, 37.8K shares and 21.9K saves when recorded: it is shared and kept far more than a short explainer is. Never reuse its wording or retell this case because it did.

**What's said:**
- 0 s: "...way to die in the world. One of the explorers ran out of strength and died near the exit of an underwater passage. But her body ended up completely blocking the way for the people behind her. In the end, the three people following her also drowned."
- 15.6 s: "This is a true story that happened in Utah in 2005."
- 20 s: "It started on an ordinary night." Then in order: a friend who'd been there before describes the cave, "They were hooked instantly", the entrance where "only one person could squeeze through at a time", a pool, a rope "left behind by earlier explorers".
- 81 s: "Then, Joseph got scared. He changed his mind and decided to wait outside. That decision saved his life."
- 91 s: a promise: "I'll tug the rope a few times so you know I made it." Then the mud that one movement turns "completely murky", the chamber "only about six feet tall", and "some of them felt dizzy. It was getting harder to breathe."
- 154 s, the last line: "What they didn't know was that the real nightmare was only beginning."

**What's seen:** one picture all the way through, a side-on cut through the rock, like a diagram. Simple figures, each known by the colour of a shirt. The rope is a line through the flooded tunnel. "UTAH · 2005" is a title on the picture. The chamber's height is a measure drawn on it ("6 ft"). An AIR bar appears at the top when the air starts to matter, and drains.

**How it's built:**
1. **The end comes first,** in fifteen seconds: who died, where, and the one detail that makes it unbearable. The viewer then watches every choice knowing what it costs.
2. **"This is a true story"**, with the place and the year, said and written, before the story starts.
3. **"It started on an ordinary night"** takes it back to the beginning, and it's told in order from there, slowly: short sentences, a pause after each.
4. **People are few, named, and told apart at a glance** (a colour each), and one of them turns back: "That decision saved his life."
5. **Every danger is planted before it bites, and drawn:** the entrance one body wide, the rope, the mud, the low roof, the air. Each is a thing on the picture, and two are meters (a measure, a bar).
6. **Each part ends on a line that opens the next.**
7. **The cut through the place is the whole look.** At every moment the viewer can see where everyone is, where the water is and where the way out is, which no camera inside the cave could show.

## What makes one

- **A place that can be cut open and drawn** (`when_where`): where, when, and what the cut shows.
- **Few people, told apart at a glance** (`people`): roles and first names only where the record gives them.
- **The worst moment, said first** (`worst_moment`): what the story opens on, in a sentence a child could follow.
- **The choices, in order** (`decisions`, 4 to 8): each small, each one anyone might have made, each seen on the cut.
- **The one that made the difference** (`the_turn`): who turned back, or what was done right, or what one thing would have saved them.
- **How it ended, and what changed because of it** (`outcome`): who lived, and what was sealed, signposted or taught afterwards. A story like this should leave the viewer knowing what to do.
- **How long the whole story runs, in one video** (`length`): the Reel's first part alone is 2 minutes 41 seconds. Say what the whole would take at that pace, and what could be cut to bring it under three minutes (see the note on Instagram below).
- **How it's known** (`how_known`): the official report, the inquest, the rescuers' own account, major reporting at the time. Not a retelling by another creator.
- **How the harm stays off the screen** (`shown_as`).

## What the kit can show

The Reel's look is closer to the kit's diagrams (`diagram/`, the systems shorts) than to its 3D people: a flat cut, simple figures that slide along it, lines, measures and bars. The 3D kit's people hold a stance and can't crawl or swim.

## The lines here

- **It's a record, so it's exact.** `how_known` is an official account where there is one (an inquest, a sheriff's or coroner's report, a rescue team's own report, an accident board), then major reporting of the time. Every name, age, time, depth and distance comes from one of them, and where accounts differ the card says so. Never from another creator's retelling.
- **The dead are shown as the Reel shows them:** the same simple figure, lying still. No blood, no wounds, no face in pain, nothing lingering. `shown_as` says what's seen at the worst moment.
- **Simple figures, never a real person's likeness.** A colour each. First names where the record gives them, and be kind to them: they're people, not a puzzle.
- **Nobody is blamed beyond what an inquiry found.** A choice is shown as one anyone might have made. The first batch got this wrong twice, both against people who can't answer, so:
  - where a report puts the failure on equipment, a ship or a company (a meter that wasn't on board, a map that was wrong), the card says that, and never calls it something the dead "didn't do";
  - where one report holds more than one finding, or two bodies differ, the card gives each, not the harshest;
  - where a court tried anyone, the card says how it ended, an acquittal as plainly as a fine;
  - a person is named beside a finding only where that finding's own source names them.
- **One source's figure isn't the figure.** Where the official record and a later feature differ (a time, a depth, a distance, how long the air lasted), the card uses the figure nearest the record and lists the others, with their sources, in `how_known`.
- **It ends on what changed:** what was sealed, signposted, taught or invented afterwards, so the viewer leaves knowing what to do.
- **A story where a child died stays out,** as `all.md` has it for every category. The owner's yes was to real deaths in general; keeping this line is the agent's caution, not yet theirs.
- **Never the Provo cave itself,** and never a case chosen because that creator told it: the Reel is how it's done, not something to remake.

## Still open

- **Which channel.** The owner hasn't said. Ideas can be found before that's settled; a video can't be made.
- **Three minutes.** Reports on Instagram (marketing blogs and one analysis, not Instagram itself, read Nov 12, 2026) agree that a Reel longer than three minutes is mostly shown to followers and not recommended to strangers, which may be why that creator posts in parts of 2:41. A new channel has no followers. So "long, and in one" may have to mean just under three minutes on Instagram, or longer somewhere else. For the owner to decide once a first script shows how long a whole story runs.

## Where to look

- Published accounts of accidents: caving and cave-rescue bodies' yearly accident reports, diving incident reports, the mountaineering clubs' accident journals, marine and mine accident boards, coroners' inquests, sheriffs' reports.
- Rescuers' own accounts and major newspapers' reporting at the time.
- A place that can be drawn as a cut: a cave, a sump, a mine, a wreck, a crevasse, a silo, a well, a capsized hull with air trapped in it.
- Rescues and survivals as well as deaths: the same build, with the turn being what was done right.
- The reference has 176 titles of this family (`run.py ideas reference survival`). His are thirty-second tellings, and a long record of a case he has touched is still a different video, but say so on the card, and prefer cases he hasn't made.

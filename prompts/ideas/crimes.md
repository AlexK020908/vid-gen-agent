# Crimes

An idea here is a crime re-staged in 3D, and what gave it away: the clue, the mistake, the test that caught them. The owner asked for this ("we can also simulate murder events"), and chose real murder cases and invented ones. The story is how it was found out. The harm is never the show.

## What makes one

1. **The first second is a moment that makes no sense yet**: a room locked from the inside, a clock stopped at the wrong time, a body of water that shouldn't have given anything up. Seen, not told.
2. **The heart of it is how it was found out, and that's a mechanism**: a clue seen, a mistake anyone could make, a science that caught them (a fingerprint, a tide table, an insect's life cycle, a receipt's time). The kit shows a mechanism well, step by step, so the break is what's shown happening.
3. **Every step is seen**, in one or two places, with few people, who hold a stance rather than act: the story's told through things, a room, the evidence. The big physical moment the star references have (`all.md`) comes from the clue or the catching, never from the harm: something racing, falling, freezing, burning down or washing away.
4. **It ends on the break, and on what happened to them**: caught, and how.
5. **An invented case plays fair**: every clue the answer needs is seen before the answer, and the break rests on something true, named in `source`. His invented stories do a little better than his median (`(fiction)` in his titles, 1.5x in the middle over 27 shorts, with far more comments), his real cases about his median: a lean worth knowing.

## The rules for a real case

Proposed on Nov 8 and waiting on the owner's yes (BUILD.md, The idea research agent): until then they stand.
- **Only a case settled in the record**: a conviction in court, known from the court's own record or major reporting of the trial. Older cases first, decades old, so no family is hurt by a re-staging they never asked for.
- **Never name or hint that anyone is guilty who wasn't convicted**, of anything. An unsolved case says only what's known, and names nobody as the one who did it.
- **Invented 3D stand-ins**, never a real person's likeness. The victim and the convicted can be named where the record names them. Be kind to the victim: they're a person, not a puzzle.
- **Nothing graphic**: the harm is told, not shown, or shown as glows and rings. No bodies, wounds or blood, and no case where a child was harmed or threatened.
- **No method someone could copy**: what happened and how it was caught, never how to do it (no poisons and doses, weapons, ways round locks or alarms). A poisoning can be told, but never which poison or how much.
- **The killer is never the hero**: the story is the catching.

## Where to look

- Famous cases solved by a clue or a new science: the histories of forensic science (the first fingerprint, the first insect evidence, the first DNA conviction), with what they cite; court records; major newspapers' reporting of the trial; museums of policing.
- For invented cases: start from a true thing a clue can rest on, then build the case round it. Read the reference's `(fiction)` and `(Murder Mystery)` shorts first (`run.py ideas reference mysteries`), and never reuse one.

## The card

Besides what every card needs (`all.md`):
- `kind`: `real` or `invented`.
- `what_happened`: 3 to 6 steps, in order, each seen.
- `the_break`: what gave it away, as it's shown happening.
- `convicted`: for a real case, who was convicted of what, by which court, and when; or "nobody", and then nobody's named as having done it. For an invented case, "invented".
- `people`: who's in it, as roles ("the night porter"), and who's named.
- `shown_as`: how the harm is shown without being graphic.

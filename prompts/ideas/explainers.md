# Explainers

An idea here is one surprising true thing, shown happening in front of you, and why. The woodpecker is the owner's star reference for it: a bird slamming its head into a tree, and why its brain isn't hurt, seen inside its head.

## What makes one

- **Something strange happens first.** The hook is a moment already under way that makes no sense yet, or an "If you..." setup that puts the viewer in it.
- **Nearly everyone would guess wrong.** `people_think` is what they'd say, and it has to be what a sensible person would really say. `truth` is what's really going on. If people would shrug and say "I knew that", it's out.
- **A small child could follow why.** `steps` are 3 to 6 links in a chain, each saying what causes the next, using the real names of things. If you can't write the chain, you don't understand it yet, or it's only a fact and not an explanation.
- **It goes somewhere after the answer.** `turn` is one more true thing, stranger or worse, that comes after. A trivia fact with nothing after it is out.
- **You can see it, and go inside it.** `inside` is how the explanation is shown: an x-ray of a body, a cut through a thing, air as moving balls.
- **Where it leads to something people built, it's an invention instead** (`prompts/ideas/inventions.md`): the owner wants a short to land on a real invention wherever one exists.

## Where to look

- **Odd things people really do, on film.** This is where the tuna cannon came from (`all.md`): working footage of farms, fisheries, factories, ports, mines, building sites, hospitals, kitchens, zoos and aquariums, where a trade does something that looks mad to an outsider and has a hard reason behind it. Search for the footage first (what's passed round as "oddly satisfying", "specialized tools", "why do they", trade and equipment makers' own videos), note the sight in one line, and only then find the reason at a source that would know: the trade's own body, the equipment maker, a paper. Also odd things animals and bodies visibly do. The footage is only the lead: the short is staged in 3D, never cut from someone else's film.
- Wikipedia's lists of common misconceptions, and of Ig Nobel winners (real studies of absurd things): `uv run python run.py ideas fetch` gets them, and `run.py ideas show <part of a page's name>` prints them. A lead isn't an idea, and most go nowhere.
- What people really type: `run.py ideas show asked`. Most of it's noise, and it says nothing about how a short would do.
- Things happening to your own body reach his top tenth a little more often than other subjects (a weak sign that held in both halves of a test).
- What the owner has seen: thirteen explainer cards were offered on Nov 7, and they never chose one. That isn't a no, but it isn't a yes either, and the taste file has them.

## The card

Besides what every card needs and the four tests' fields, `silent`, `mistake`, `stake` and `seen` (`all.md`): `people_think`, `truth`, `steps` (3 to 6), `turn` and `inside`, as above.

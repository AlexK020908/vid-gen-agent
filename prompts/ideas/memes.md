# Memes

An idea here is a joke told as a reference short: someone the viewer knows, dropped into a story everyone knows, every step built from something they're really known for, ending on a twist. The owner sent two as the bar on Nov 8, 2026 ("We want ot be able to make memes like this"), both made by others in the reference's style:

- **A rapper stops Adam and Eve eating the forbidden fruit.** "Did Carti stop Adam and Eve from eating the forbidden fruit?" He hides the apple in his Rick Owens boot; the snake whispers, and he hisses his catchphrase back; he does his baby voice and Adam and Eve cover their ears; he spreads a black cape and hundreds of bats lift him up the tree; the snake bares its fangs, and he bares bigger ones; he drinks the apple dry and hands it back: "Problem solved." Eve stares at the two holes: "You weren't supposed to eat it either." "I drank it." Every step is something he's known for (the boots, the catchphrase, the voice, the vampire image he's made for himself), and each does a job in the story.
- **A streamer is taken for a monster.** "What if Cuffem was actually a monster?" Scientists scan his face and scream "We found it!"; a giant monster rises from the sea, the army surrounds it, and it kneels to him. He watched it on stream, protesting how it drew him, and the clip of him reacting is how it spread. It turns on how he looks, so under rule 2 below it's the kind this channel doesn't make.

What the second shows about how these travel: the person in one may see it and react, and their audience comes with them. That's luck, not a plan, and it only happens when they'd laugh.

## What makes one

- **Someone millions would know from their name and their look**, and **two to four things they're known for that anyone who knows them would recognise**: a catchphrase, what they wear, how they talk, a famous moment, the image they've made for themselves. `known_for` lists them, each with where it's known from.
- **A story everyone knows, or an impossible what-if**: Eden, the ark, the Trojan horse, the moon landing, a monster from the sea. Its goal is plain from the first second (stop them eating the apple), and the person reaches it their own way.
- **Every step is one of the things they're known for, seen doing a job in the story**: the boot hides the apple, and the cape and the bats lift him up the tree. A step that's only a costume, or a joke the viewer has to be told, is out.
- **It's told straight**, as the reference tells a true story: "One night...", "until suddenly...", short lines with one thing happening in each, and a big caption word or two a shot.
- **It ends on a twist that turns the goal around** (`punchline`): he stopped them eating it by drinking it.
- **It's plainly made up**: impossible, never something they might really have done or said, and never anything that could pass as news.

## The rules (proposed Nov 8, waiting on the owner's explicit yes)

Every other kind uses invented people only. These name real ones, so these rules hold for every meme. The owner answered the question with "go", which isn't taken as a yes to putting real people in videos: they're asked again in so many words.

1. **Public figures only**: people famous for what they do in public (streamers, rappers, athletes, creators), adults, never a private person.
2. **The joke comes from what they're known for doing and saying, never from who they are**: not their race, religion, looks or body, health, family or sexuality.
3. **Nothing sexual, no harm done to them, and nothing about a real scandal, crime, court case or death.**
4. **Their face isn't copied**: they're known by name, hair, clothes and their things, on an invented face, never one made from photos of them. Their lines are said by our narrator, since nobody's voice is ever cloned.
5. **The caption says it's a parody.**

The owner then said, on Nov 8: "for memes ill explecitly describe what i want". Until they have, don't bring cards for this kind, and expect this brief to change with what they describe. After that, a card that breaks any of its rules is dropped, however funny it is.

## Where to look

- **Who's being talked about now, and what they're known for**: Know Your Meme's pages on people and catchphrases (with what they cite), their Wikipedia pages, their interviews and their own posts.
- **Famous stories**: the best-known scenes of the Bible, Greek myths, fairy tales and famous moments in history. The reference's made-up "what if"s show the kind of setup that works, but never use his stories.
- His `(fiction)` and "what if" shorts do a little better than his median (`run.py ideas reference memes`): a weak lean, but they're the nearest kin he has.

## The card

Besides what every card needs (`all.md`):
- `who`: the person, as the channel would name them.
- `known_for`: 2 to 4 things, each one they're really known for, with where it's known from.
- `story`: the famous story or the what-if, and its goal.
- `steps`: 4 to 8, each one of their things doing a job in the story, seen.
- `punchline`: the twist line, as it's said.

`needs` lists the person as an invented face with their hair, clothes and things. `source` and `source_url` are for what they're known for: a page that shows it.

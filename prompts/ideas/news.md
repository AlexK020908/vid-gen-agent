# News, walked through

An idea here is one thing that really happened lately, and how it happened, step by step, told to someone with no experience of the subject. The owner asked for this on Nov 11, 2026: "could be news as well, like how crypto was hacked by a hacker recently from north korea then walk though how it happened like how u would explain to a person with no experience in crypto". And of every category that day: "each has to have a good hook".

## What makes one

1. **The headline is the hook, and it's a number or a thing, not a subject.** "Someone stole a billion and a half dollars without touching a bank." The first second shows what was taken, broken or done, big in the middle, and it has to stop someone with the sound off (`silent`).
2. **Something a viewer feels is at stake** (`stake`): people's money, a city without power, a crew, whether it could happen to them.
3. **It's told to a newcomer** (`newcomer`). Before any step, say in one line what the viewer has to know and the everyday thing that stands for it: a crypto wallet is a safe that opens for whoever holds one long password; signing is turning the key. Every term in the short gets a stand-in like that the first time it's said, and keeps it. If a step can't be given one, the idea isn't ready.
4. **How it happened is a chain a child could follow** (`steps`, 4 to 7): each step is one thing someone did or one thing that gave way, seen happening, and each causes the next. The step where it turns, the one mistake or the one clever move, is the moment the short builds to.
5. **It ends on what changed or what it teaches** (`lesson`): how they were caught or weren't, what was fixed, the one habit that would have stopped it.
6. **It's recent, and it's settled enough to tell** (`when`): within about the last twelve months, with the facts established by someone who would know. A story still changing by the day waits.

## How it's told about real people

- **Who did it is said the way the record says it, and no firmer** (`said_by`): "the FBI says a North Korean group did it", never "North Korea did it" as our own claim. Name the body that said it and link its statement. Where nobody official has said, the short says nobody knows.
- No private person is named, shown or hinted at. A company or an agency can be named where the record names it. People are invented 3D stand-ins with no real person's face.
- Nobody is accused of a crime unless a court has convicted them or an official body has publicly attributed it, and then in that body's words.
- **Nothing that works as instructions.** The short explains the mechanism at the level a newspaper would: what was tricked, what was trusted that shouldn't have been. Never the commands, the tools, the code or the order of steps someone could follow to do it again.
- Nothing graphic. No event where people were killed or hurt less than a year ago, no war, no elections, no court case still being tried.

## Where to look

- Official statements and reports first: a national agency (the FBI, a transport safety board, a regulator), the company's own post-mortem, a court filing. Then the careful trade press that explains them.
- What makes a good one: thefts and hacks, a big outage, a scam many people fell for, something that broke or fell and its inquiry, a record set, a rescue.
- Staging: money and data are shown as things (a safe, keys, glowing balls moving between boxes), machines as models that open; a process inside a network can be a live diagram on the systems channel instead (`tech.md`). `needs` says which.

## The card

Besides what every card needs (`all.md`): `silent` and `stake` (the four tests), `happened` (what happened, in two sentences), `when` (the date, and the date of the source that settles it), `newcomer` (what a viewer must know, and the everyday stand-in for each term), `steps` (4 to 7), `lesson`, and `said_by` (who attributes it to whom, in their words, with the link).

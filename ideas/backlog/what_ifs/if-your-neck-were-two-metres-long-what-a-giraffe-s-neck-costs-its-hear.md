# If your neck were two metres long: what a giraffe's neck costs its heart and its breath

## The first second

**Heard:** If your neck were two metres long, you might think you could look over any wall and eat from the top of the tree.

**Seen:** A person standing by a front door in an ordinary street, their neck already rising like a periscope: in the first second the head passes the top of the door frame, by two seconds it is level with the upstairs window, and a ruler drawn up the neck reads 2 m.

## Why someone would stop

- **With the sound off:** A person in an ordinary street whose neck is growing like a periscope, the head rising past the door frame toward the upstairs window. No words are needed to ask what is happening to them.
- **What grabs them:** The wish is the height: the view over every wall and the top of every tree. It turns on you because the head is now two metres above the heart and the lungs: the height that gives the view is the height the blood and the breath can't climb.
- **What's at stake:** Your own brain going without blood and your lungs short of fresh air: by the sum, you'd faint standing up straight.
- **The answer you watch:** Used grey air, most or all of a breath of it, shuttling up and down a windpipe as long as a door while little or none of the fresh blue air arrives; a red column of blood climbing the neck and stopping short of the head; the pressure badge going from 90 toward the 215 it would need; then the giraffe's thick heart doing it.

## How it goes

**Wish:** Your head looks over a three-metre garden wall into the next garden, then bites an apple straight off the top branch of a tree, with both feet on the ground. Held about four seconds before anything goes wrong.

**Number:** A neck 2 m long (a giraffe's is about 2 m; sources give 1.8 to 2.4). It drives two quantities. Blood: lifting blood 2 m takes about 155 mmHg of pressure, more than the 70 to 110 mmHg a human heart keeps up. Air: a windpipe 2 m long holds about half a litre (roughly 0.35 to 0.6 by the person), most or all of a resting breath.

**Law:** Pressure falls with height in any fluid (P = height x density x g), and the air left in the airways is dead space that never reaches the lungs.

**Real thing:** The giraffe. Its neck is why its blood pressure at the heart is about twice ours and why its heart is a thick wall round a small chamber; its windpipe is narrower than in other mammals its size (the study measures that and does not say why); and its neck is why the skin of its legs is tight enough to help stop that pressure pushing fluid out into them (Hargens 1987). The viewer leaves knowing what a long neck costs the animal that has one.

**Sums:** Ours, to be tested apart from the sources: (1) 2 m x 1,050 kg/m3 x 9.81 m/s2 = 20,600 Pa; 20,600 / 133.3 = 154.5 mmHg; 77 mmHg per metre. (2) 90 mmHg / 77 = 1.16 m. (3) 60 + 155 = 215 mmHg needed at the heart; the measured giraffe figure, 224, is the source's. (4) Windpipe: pi x 0.9 cm x 0.9 cm = 2.5 cm2, x 200 cm = 509 mL; airways 1.5 to 2 cm across: 1.8 to 3.1 cm2, x 200 cm = 350 to 630 mL.

**Unsure:** The 'column stops 1.2 m up' picture is the plain reading of the law.

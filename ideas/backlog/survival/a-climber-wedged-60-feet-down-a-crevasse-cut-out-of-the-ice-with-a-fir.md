## How it goes

**When where:** The Kahiltna Glacier at 7,800 feet on the West Buttress route of Denali, Alaska, USA, from about 11:30 p.m. on 4 June to 3:35 p.m. on 5 June 2017. The cut shows the snow surface, the snow bridge, and the crevasse narrowing downwards to about a foot wide near where he stuck.

**People:** Martin, 38, from Slovakia (the National Park Service's release names him as Martin Takac; the climbing club's report leaves his name out), in one colour; his partner on snowshoes; the mountain guides camped nearby who reached him first; park rangers and volunteers; a helicopter pilot. Four colours at most.

**Worst moment:** A climber fell into a crack in a glacier and stuck so tightly where it narrowed that no rope could pull him out. Rescuers had to cut the ice away from around him while he grew colder for sixteen hours.

**The turn:** One thing would have stopped it: a rope to his partner, which the climbing club's report calls the only way to prevent an accident like this. Two things saved him: people camped close enough to reach him within the hour and clip him in before he could slip deeper, and rescuers who stopped trying with the usual tools and sent for a fire brigade's.

**Outcome:** He lived. The climbing club's report says he was in intensive care for several days, mainly for the cold, with a nerve injury in one arm; the medical journal's release says he went home after 14 days and recovered fully. The park said it was the second big crevasse rescue of an unroped climber that season, and told climbers to travel roped, on skis or snowshoes. The journal's release says Denali's rangers now carry a kit for this in their rescue aircraft: the pneumatic chisel, a tripod and winch, and rewarming gear; the release gives no date for this and does not say it was because of this rescue. What a viewer takes away: a snow bridge that held for everyone else can still drop you, so rope up; and don't stop working on someone who is very cold.

I read the release, not the paper. Where they differ: the fall is 40 feet in the park's release, about 60 feet in the club's report and about 20 metres in the journal's; the park says the work took about 14 hours, the club's title says 15 and its text and the journal say about 16; the park says the fall was reported about 1:30 a.m., the club puts it at 11:30 p.m. The park's own district ranger said four days later that the first estimate was off, more like sixty feet (KTNA, 9 June 2017), so 60 feet is the park's corrected figure: https://ktna.org/denali-report-for-june-9-2017-crevasse-rescue/ (opened by the fact-checker). Seen only in a search summary, so not on the card: that the ice walls where he stuck were 18 to 23 centimetres apart.

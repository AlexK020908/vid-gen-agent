## How it goes

**When where:** The Riesending shaft cave in the Untersberg, near Bischofswiesen and Berchtesgaden, Bavaria, Germany, 8 to 19 June 2014. It is Germany's deepest and longest cave: 1,148 metres deep and about 20 kilometres long, with its entrance at about 1,840 metres up the mountain. The accident was at about 1,000 metres depth. The cut shows the whole height of it: the entrance, the big shafts, the camps on the way, and the bottom.

**People:** Johann (Johann Westhauser, named in the rescuers' own release), in one colour; his two companions, whom the doctors' account doesn't name: the one who stayed and the one who climbed out; the cave doctors; and the stretcher teams, 14 to 20 rescuers at a time, drawn as a few figures in one colour. Over the whole operation 202 cave rescuers went underground, 175 of them from Austria, Switzerland, Italy and Croatia.

**Worst moment:** A falling rock knocked a caver unconscious a kilometre underground, and it was three and a half days before a doctor could reach him.

**The turn:** He had a helmet on, and there were three of them. The block hit the helmet, not his bare head; one friend could stay with him while the other got out and raised the alarm twelve hours after the accident. Both facts are in the doctors' account. That three is the fewest for a trip like this is ordinary caving advice and is my addition, not theirs.

**Outcome:** He lived. Hospital found a severe brain injury with bleeding and broken bones in the face, and the doctors who treated him underground call it remarkable that he came through without the usual intensive care or surgery; the Berchtesgaden tourist office's page says he has fully recovered. On 2 July 2014 the municipality of Bischofswiesen banned going into the cave: the entrance is closed and locked, and an exception needs a research reason and proof of rescue insurance. Bavaria's parliament set aside 523,700 euros in 2015 for rescue costs that could not be recovered. The head of the European cave rescuers said after it that rescuers should be alerted sooner and that working across borders should be made easier. What a viewer takes away: helmet on, never fewer than three, and why a cave like this is locked.

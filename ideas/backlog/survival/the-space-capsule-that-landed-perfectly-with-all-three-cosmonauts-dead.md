## How it goes

**When where:** The descent capsule of Soyuz 11, coming home from Salyut, the first space station, on 30 June 1971. The valve opened about 100 miles above the Earth, and the capsule landed by itself about 200 kilometres (125 miles) east of Zhezkazgan in Kazakhstan. The cut shows the capsule with three seats side by side and one small valve in its wall, and around it the spacecraft's other two parts coming away.

**People:** Three cosmonauts, named in NASA's history: Georgi (Georgi Dobrovolski, the commander), Vladislav (Vladislav Volkov, the only one who had flown before) and Viktor (Viktor Patsayev, who turned 38 on the station). A colour each. A voice on the ground, and the recovery crew.

**Worst moment:** Three cosmonauts came home from the first space station in a capsule that did everything right except for one small valve. It opened in space, long before it should have: they were unconscious within a minute, and the air was gone in about two.

**The turn:** One thing would have saved them: a pressure suit each. NASA's history says that without suits they had no hope of surviving. It was not the crew's choice: the designers had refused suits (Kamanin's diary, by Encyclopedia Astronautica); the usual account is that three men in suits didn't fit, and NASA's medical office notes that the next crews flew two at a time so that both could wear one.

**Outcome:** All three died. Flights stopped while the Soyuz was redesigned. The next crews were two, not three, so both could wear a suit; a new pressure suit, the Sokol, was brought in, and later made small enough for three to fly again. NASA's history says every cosmonaut since has worn a suit for launch and landing on a Soyuz. The three were given a state funeral in Red Square, and a month later the crew of Apollo 15 left a plaque on the Moon to fallen astronauts and cosmonauts, these three among them. What a viewer takes away: why astronauts wear a suit inside a sealed spacecraft at all.

# Unsolved: the Navy blimp that came home in 1942 with nobody aboard

- **Kind:** mysteries, built as `mystery`
- **Your answer so far:** not shown to you yet

## The first second

**Heard:** A Navy blimp lands on a street, door open, nobody inside.

**Seen:** A grey Navy blimp, sagging in the middle into a V, settling onto a street of houses, its cabin door hanging open and both seats empty.

## Why someone would stop

- **With the sound off:** A sagging blimp coming down between houses with an open door and empty seats: a viewer sees an aircraft with nobody flying it, with no word needed.
- **What grabs them:** It looks like a mistake: an aircraft that has flown itself home. A stranger would think the crew must have jumped or crashed, and the cabin shows neither.
- **What's at stake:** Two men, Lt. Ernest Cody and Ensign Charles Adams, who took off at 6:03 that morning and were never seen again.
- **The answer you watch:** Each explanation is tested on things in the cabin: the open door, the dry floor, the parachutes and raft still stowed, the radio that works. None is a witness's word.

## How it goes

**Kind:** real

**Strange:** A patrol blimp that took off with two men drifts in from the sea and comes down in a street in Daly City, California, with its cabin empty and nothing wrong with it.

**Answer:** Three explanations, each staged and tested. One: the blimp dipped into the sea and a wave took them; the dry, dusty floor rules it out. Two: they left on purpose, or were taken off by a vessel; the parachutes, raft and secret briefcase left aboard, and the working radio, fit neither, and no vessel was ever found. Three, the Navy's own guess: one man leaned out of the open door to drop a smoke light on the oil slick and slipped, the other tried to haul him back, and both fell; the blimp, suddenly two men lighter, rose and drifted home. That fits the door, the missing lights, the life jackets and the climb. It doesn't fit the last clue: the blimp circled that slick low down for close to an hour in view of a fishing boat and a cargo ship, and nobody aboard either reported seeing anyone fall.

**Solved:** Unsolved. The Navy board of investigation (1942) reached no cause, and both men were declared dead a year later. Still argued: whether both fell from the open door, and if so why two ships watching saw nothing and no call was made.

**People:** Two airmen, as empty seats and two life jackets (named in the record: Lt. Ernest DeWitt Cody and Ensign Charles Adams); two surf fishermen who grab the blimp's ropes on the beach and look in.

**Shown as:** Nothing hurtful shown. The men are never seen falling: the Navy's guess is staged with the open door, a smoke light dropping and the blimp jumping upward, and the loss is told in words.

1. The cabin floor and the lower fin are bone dry, with dust still on them: the blimp never touched the sea, so no wave took the men out.
2. All three parachutes and the life raft are still stowed, and the weighted briefcase of secret papers, which orders said to throw overboard in an emergency, is still there: they didn't bail out, and they weren't expecting trouble.
3. The two life jackets are gone: crews wore them over water as a rule, so the men were wearing theirs wherever they went.
4. The radio works and is set to the right frequency, and no call for help was ever sent: whatever happened was too quick for a call, or neither man could get back to the set.
5. The door is found latched open, and two of the five floating smoke lights are missing: someone had the door open over the oil slick they'd reported at 7:38 to drop markers. (Whether the door was latched open by the crew or by the first people to reach the wreck has been argued.)

**The ending:** The Navy's board could only say what didn't happen: not shot down, not burned, never in the water. The cabin, with its door, is in a museum in Florida today, and the last clue is still open: a working radio, and no call.

## To settle before it's written

- 'About an hour' circling the slick, not 'close to an hour' (sources say nearly, and more than, an hour).
- 'The bilge under the cabin and the lower fin are bone dry', not the cabin floor.
- Cut 'weighted' from the briefcase and 'with its door' from the ending: no source says either.
- The two ships are what was in view, not the open clue (nobody reporting a fall is a witness's say-so). End on the working radio and no call.

## Where it's known from

# Unsolved: the three lighthouse keepers who vanished from a rock in 1900

- **Kind:** mysteries, built as `mystery`
- **Your answer so far:** not shown to you yet

## The first second

**Heard:** The lighthouse is dark, and one keeper's coat is still on its peg.

**Seen:** A lighthouse on a sea cliff at dusk with its lamp dark, and inside the door three coat pegs: two bare, one with an oilskin coat still hanging.

## Why someone would stop

- **With the sound off:** A dark lighthouse over a wild sea, then three pegs with one coat left: a viewer sees a light that should be burning and a man who went out into a storm without his coat.
- **What grabs them:** It looks like a mistake: the one building that must never go dark is dark, and nothing inside is out of place. A stranger would look for a fight or a wreck, and there's no sign of either.
- **What's at stake:** Three men, James Ducat, Thomas Marshall and Donald McArthur, alone on the rock, and every ship that passed it in the dark.
- **The answer you watch:** A slate with its last chalked note, pegs and boots, a shut gate, twisted iron rails and a box torn from a cliff 110 feet up: each explanation is checked against those.

## How it goes

**Kind:** real

**Strange:** A lighthouse on a rock in the Atlantic, twenty miles from anyone, has its lamp out. The gate and the door are shut, the lamps are cleaned and filled, the kitchen is tidy, and all three keepers are gone.

**Answer:** Three explanations, each staged on the cliff. One, the official one (Superintendent Robert Muirhead, January 1901): two men in oilskins go down to make the rope box fast, and a great sea climbs the cliff and takes them. It fits the oilskins and the damage, and says nothing of the third man, whose rules said one keeper must always stay with the light. Two: the third man sees big seas coming, runs down to warn them without his coat, and is taken with them. It fits the coat on the peg. Three: one man is washed off the landing, and the other two are lost trying to reach him. Against two and three is the same small thing: a man running for his friends' lives, and the door and the gate are shut behind him.

**Solved:** Unsolved. The Northern Lighthouse Board's inquiry (Muirhead's report, 8 January 1901) concluded the men were swept away by an unexpectedly large sea at the west landing. Still argued: why the third man left the light, against the rules and without his oilskins, and who shut the door and gate.

**People:** Three keepers, seen only as their things: boots, oilskins, a slate (named in the record: James Ducat, Thomas Marshall, Donald McArthur), and the relief keeper who lands on 26 December and finds the place empty (Joseph Moore).

**Shown as:** Nothing hurtful shown. The sea is shown climbing an empty cliff and tearing out the box; when an explanation is staged the men are small figures in oilskins on the landing, and the picture cuts to the bare landing before the wave arrives. Their deaths are told.

1. The slate in the kitchen: the last note is for 9 in the morning of 15 December, with the lamp cleaned and filled. That night a passing steamer found the light out: it happened that day, after the morning's work was done.
2. The pegs: two sets of oilskins and sea boots are gone, which the keepers wore only to go down to the landings. The third set is still hanging: the third man went out in his shirt sleeves.
3. The gate and the outer door are shut: nobody left in a way that broke anything, and whoever went out last closed up behind him.
4. Down at the west landing: iron railings twisted, a block of stone of more than a ton pushed out of place, and a box of ropes kept in a cleft about 110 feet above the sea torn out and gone, with turf ripped off the clifftop 200 feet up: the sea had reached heights where a man would think himself safe.

**The ending:** No one was ever found. The table laid for a meal, the fallen chair and the logbook full of fear that the story is usually told with were all added later by writers: the record has only the slate, the pegs and the cliff. And the shut gate is still unexplained.

## To settle before it's written

- Explanation one, as the 1901 report really says: 'all three go down to make the rope box fast, and a great sea climbs the cliff and takes them. It fits the waterproofs and the damage; it doesn't say why the third man went without his coat, or why the light was left with nobody in it.'
- Clue 2, as the record has it: 'Two men's sea boots and waterproofs are gone, which the keepers wore only to go down to the landings. The third man's everyday coat is still there: he went out in his shirt sleeves.' The hook still holds.
- The turf ripped at 200 feet is from later accounts, not the report. Use the report's own detail instead: a lifebuoy torn from the railings 110 feet above the sea.
- End on the third man's coat and all three leaving the light. The shut gate as 'the unexplained clue' is our own reasoning, and no source treats it that way.
- Source for the gate, doors, box and conclusion: the Northern Lighthouse Board, https://www.nlb.org.uk/history/flannan-isles/

## Where it's known from

National Records of Scotland (which holds the Northern Lighthouse Board's file, NLC3/1/1), 'The Flannan Islands Lighthouse: A Disappearance': the three keepers and Moore, the Hesperus landing on 26 December 1900, the slate entries to 9 am on 15 December, lamps trimmed and kitchen tidy, the two sets of outdoor clothing gone and worn only for the landings, the twisted railing and the stone of more than 20 hundredweight, and Muirhead's conclusion of a large roller. From a summary (Wikipedia, citing Moore's and Muirhead's reports and Haswell-Smith 2004): the shut gate and door, the third set of oilskins left behind, the box at about 110 feet, the turf at 200 feet, the three later explanations, and that the dramatic logbook entries are a later invention (Mike Dash, Fortean Times). The full text of Muirhead's report was not read.

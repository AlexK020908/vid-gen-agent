## How it goes

**Kind:** invented

**Strange:** The helmsman kept the needle on north all night under cloud, on a course the chart shows passing well clear of the reef, and the ship ran straight onto it.

**Answer:** From above and inside: the iron rails pull the ship's compass needle about 15 degrees off. The helmsman keeps that pulled needle on N, so all night the ship sails 15 degrees off its line, further from it every hour, until it reaches the reef.

**Solved:** invented

**People:** The helmsman and the captain, who react. Nobody's named.

**Shown as:** Nothing hurtful: the ship sticks fast on the reef and the crew stay aboard, safe, until help comes at dawn.

1. The chart: the pencilled course runs well clear of the reef, and the log shows north hour after hour: the plan was right, and he steered it.
2. At dawn the captain rows the ship's boat a little way off, and his hand compass there points about 15 degrees away from where the ship's compass points: the ship's compass is the one that's wrong.
3. In the hold, stacked against the starboard side just below and beside the ship's compass: a new cargo of iron rails, loaded the day before they sailed.
4. Bring a spanner near the hand compass and the needle swings after it: iron pulls a compass needle.

**The ending:** That's why a ship's compass stand carries iron balls and hidden magnets, Kelvin's design patented in 1876, set to cancel the ship's own iron, and why the compass is checked again on every heading whenever iron cargo comes aboard.

## Where it's known from

Royal Museums Greenwich on Thomson's (Kelvin's) binnacle, patented 1876: the iron in ships made compass needles deviate, and its iron spheres and magnets counteract the ship's iron; Matthew Flinders tied his compass errors to his cargo of iron cannonballs in 1801 (Bruce County Museum & Cultural Centre, Ontario); Thomson's US patent 210,069 (1878): soft-iron globes for the quadrantal error, magnets for the semicircular and heeling errors; Bowditch, American Practical Navigator ch. 6: magnetic cargo causes deviation; IRClass Technical Circular 44/2016: check compasses after carrying magnetic cargo.

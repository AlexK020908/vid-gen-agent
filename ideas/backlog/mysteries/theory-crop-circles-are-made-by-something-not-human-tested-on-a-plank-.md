# Theory: crop circles are made by something not human, tested on a plank and a rope

- **Kind:** mysteries, built as `mystery`
- **Your answer so far:** not shown to you yet

## The first second

**Heard:** Overnight, a perfect giant circle is pressed flat into a wheat field.

**Seen:** A wheat field at dawn from high above, with a perfect circle and rings pressed flat into it, the stalks swirled like water down a drain, and a farmer standing small at its edge.

## Why someone would stop

- **With the sound off:** A huge exact pattern in a field seen from above, with a tiny figure beside it for size: it looks made, and far too big and too neat for anyone to have made in a night.
- **What grabs them:** It looks impossible more than wrong: a drawing the size of a house, done in the dark, with no lines or plans in sight. A stranger would say no person could do that, which is just what the theory says.
- **What's at stake:** A farmer's crop flattened before harvest, and for thirteen summers a real question nobody could answer: who, or what, is doing this.
- **The answer you watch:** A swirl of bent stalks, a map of where the circles turn up, a plank on a rope under a man's foot, a wire loop on a cap, and an expert kneeling in a circle two pensioners made: all watched.

## How it goes

**Kind:** real

**Strange:** A farmer finds a perfect circle in his standing wheat that wasn't there the evening before: every stalk bent flat at the foot and swirled one way, none cut, and no sign of who did it.

**Answer:** The theory is told as a theory: some believe the circles are made by visitors from elsewhere or by an unknown force of nature, a belief that grew through the 1980s as the patterns did. The evidence for it is the patterns themselves: their size, their neatness, their arriving in a night. The evidence against is staged in the field: two men, a plank, a rope and a cap make the same swirl of bent stalks in the dark, and the man best placed to tell the difference can't. Others took it up after them, with tapes and garden rollers, which is why the patterns grew so elaborate.

**Solved:** Solved, firmly: every tested case is consistent with people making them. Best primary evidence against the theory: Bower and Chorley's demonstration of September 1991, reported at the time (Today, 9 Sept 1991; New York Times, 10 Sept 1991), in which the investigator Pat Delgado declared their circle genuine. What remains: who made most individual circles is unknown, and W. C. Levengood's 1994 report of altered stalks has not been independently replicated.

**People:** A farmer who reacts; two older men with a plank and a rope (named in the record, by their own account: Doug Bower and Dave Chorley, both now dead); an investigator who kneels in the circle (Pat Delgado, named in the reports of the time).

**Shown as:** Nothing hurtful. The two men are shown as pranksters by their own telling, not accused of anything; the investigator is shown examining the circle and standing up surprised, without mockery.

1. Close in: the stalks are bent over at the foot and laid in a swirl, not cut or burnt: something pressed them down and went round as it did.
2. A map of England with a dot for every circle: they crowd along roads and near towns and old monuments. In one year nearly half were within 15 km of the stone circle at Avebury. Whatever makes them likes a lay-by. (One study, of a single year's circles.)
3. 1991: two men in their sixties, Doug Bower and Dave Chorley, tell a newspaper they've been doing it since the 1970s, and show how. A plank with a rope looped from each end, held up by the hands and pressed down by one foot, step after step, round a centre stake; and a wire loop on a cap to sight a straight line.
4. The test: they make a fresh circle for the newspaper, and a leading believer in the unexplained is brought to look at it. He kneels in it and pronounces it genuine, one no human could have made. Then he's told.

**The ending:** The two said they made more than two hundred. Circles still appear in English fields every summer, their makers mostly unknown, since they work at night and don't sign them. One claim is still argued: a 1994 paper reported changes in the stalks inside some circles, and nobody independent has repeated it.

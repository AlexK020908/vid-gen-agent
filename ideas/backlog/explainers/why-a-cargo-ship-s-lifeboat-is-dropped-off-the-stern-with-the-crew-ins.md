# Why a cargo ship's lifeboat is dropped off the stern with the crew inside

## How it goes

**People think:** That a lifeboat should be lowered gently to the water on ropes, and that dropping a boat full of people from the height of a building is how you'd hurt them.

**Truth:** A ship full of ore or grain can go down too fast for boats to be lowered on ropes. So bulk carriers built since 2006 carry one enclosed lifeboat on a sloping ramp at the stern, pointing down at the sea. The crew strap into high-backed seats, it is released from inside, and it slides off and falls. It lands well away from the ship and is already moving away from it, without its engine.

**Inside:** The boat cut open along its length on the ramp: rows of high-backed seats, the crew strapped in, one hand on a release lever. Then the drop seen from the side with a dotted path from the ramp to the water and a distance line from the ship's stern to the splash, growing as the boat runs on. Beside it, for contrast, an open boat hanging on two ropes against the tilting side of the ship, swinging, a clock badge running.

1. A bulk carrier with a hull full of heavy cargo can sink too quickly for ordinary lifeboats to be swung out and lowered on ropes.
2. So its lifeboat sits on a ramp at the stern, sloping down toward the sea. The crew climb in, shut the hatch and strap into seats with backs a metre high.
3. A release worked from inside lets it go. It slides down the ramp and falls: on one bulk carrier, about 19 metres, the height of a six-storey building.
4. It hits the water nose first, well behind the ship, still carrying the speed of the slide and the fall.
5. That speed takes it on away from the ship by itself, before the engine is even needed: by the rules it must be moving forward the moment it lands and must not touch the ship.

**The turn:** The fall is only safe sitting down and strapped in. In September 2023, on the bulk carrier Golden Zhejiang at anchor off British Columbia, an engineer was working inside the boat on a routine check when the release was set off by mistake. The boat dropped about 19 metres with him in it and not in a seat, and he was seriously hurt. Canada's safety board reported on it in November 2025.

**The ending:** The orange boat bobs up a long way behind the ship and motors off, a white wake behind it, while the ship's stern settles lower. Said: lowering a boat takes time a sinking ship may not have. Dropping it takes seconds, and the fall itself carries it clear.

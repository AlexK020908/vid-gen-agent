## How it goes

**People think:** That taking a newborn from its mother is the worst thing you could do to it, and that a mother with twins would simply raise both.

**Truth:** A panda has twins about half the time, but she can hold and nurse only one newborn at a time, and the other is left. So keepers put one twin in a warm incubator and leave her the other, and every few hours they swap them. She only ever has one cub to hold, and both get her milk.

**Inside:** The den seen from above with two places lit: the mother's arms and the incubator. A cub in each, a small milk badge filling beside whichever cub is with the mother and a warm glow round the one in the incubator; a clock badge turns, the cubs change places, and the other cub's milk badge fills. Two weight badges climb together.

1. A giant panda gives birth to twins about half the time, and each newborn is tiny: about 90 to 140 grams, pink and helpless.
2. A newborn has to be held against her and put in place to feed. She can do that for one cub, and the second is left lying apart.
3. So keepers lift one cub away and put it in an incubator, where it's kept warm and fed from a bottle.
4. A few hours later they swap: the cub from the incubator goes to the mother, and the one she was holding goes to the incubator.
5. The swap is repeated day and night, so she only ever has one cub to hold, and both cubs get their turn at her milk and both grow.

**The turn:** It only works if she lets go. At the Smithsonian's National Zoo in 2015 the mother, Mei Xiang, sometimes would not put down the cub she was holding, and the keepers could only wait with the other one in the incubator. The zoo's own words: much of their method 'will be dictated by Mei Xiang'.

**The ending:** Two round black-and-white cubs at two months old, side by side, one heavier than the other by a little, their mother behind them holding one. Said: she never raised two cubs at once. She raised one at a time, twice, and never knew.

## Where it's known from

Smithsonian's National Zoo, update of 23 Aug 2015 on Mei Xiang's twins: 'Giant pandas give birth to twins approximately 50 percent of the time'; cubs weighed 138 g and 86.3 g; 'The panda team will alternately swap the cubs, allowing one to nurse and spend time with Mei Xiang' while the other is kept warm in an incubator and bottle-fed; 'Much of their methods will be dictated by Mei Xiang'. Why one twin is usually lost: Rebecca Snyder, Zoo Atlanta's curator of mammals, in National Geographic, 17 Jul 2013: 'Sometimes they're just not capable of getting them positioned and one of them fails'; that zoo swapped its twins 'every few hours'. That Mei Xiang at times would not put a cub down, delaying swaps, is from news reports of Aug 2015 (NBC Washington, CBS) read as search summaries.

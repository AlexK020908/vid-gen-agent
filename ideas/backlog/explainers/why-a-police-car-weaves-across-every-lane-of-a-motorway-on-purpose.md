# Why a police car weaves across every lane of a motorway on purpose

- **Kind:** explainers, built as `why_it_happens`
- **Your answer so far:** not shown to you yet

## The first second

**Heard:** A police car is swerving across every lane of the freeway, on purpose.

**Seen:** Seen from above and behind: a patrol car with its lights on, slanting across five lanes of freeway in a zigzag, a wall of cars held back behind it and the road ahead of it empty.

## Why someone would stop

- **With the sound off:** A police car with its lights on zigzagging across all five lanes of a freeway, a wall of cars stuck behind it and empty road in front.
- **What grabs them:** The one driver who should know better is swerving all over the road like a drunk.
- **What's at stake:** The person who has to walk out into a freeway lane, and every driver about to hit a ladder at 65 miles an hour.
- **The answer you watch:** Cars bunching up behind the weaving patrol car while the cars ahead pull away, an empty stretch of road growing between them, and the ladder dragged off inside it.

## How it goes

**People think:** That the officer is drunk, showing off or chasing someone. And that if something needed clearing off a freeway, you'd close the freeway.

**Truth:** It's called a traffic break. Something is lying in a lane further on, and nobody can walk out into traffic moving at freeway speed to fetch it. So a patrol car gets in front of the traffic well before the spot, switches its lights on and weaves from side to side across all the lanes, so that no driver can get past and every car behind slows to its speed. That opens a stretch of empty road ahead, and in it the hazard is cleared.

**Inside:** The freeway from high above, like a board: the ladder marked in one lane, a block of cars ahead of the patrol car pulling away, a block behind it held to its speed, and the empty stretch between them growing, with a length badge counting up. A small figure crosses the lanes inside the empty stretch and drags the ladder off. Then the same scene with one car passing the patrol car: it shoots into the empty stretch toward the figure, with a cross over it.

1. A ladder falls off a truck and lies in a lane of a freeway where every car is doing 65 miles an hour: nobody can step out to fetch it.
2. Well back from the ladder, a patrol car switches on its lights and weaves from side to side across all the lanes.
3. No driver can get past it, so every car behind slows down to the patrol car's speed.
4. The cars that were already ahead of it carry on at full speed and pull away, so an empty stretch of road opens between them and the patrol car.
5. In that empty stretch the ladder is dragged off to the side of the road.
6. The patrol car's lights go off and it moves aside, and the traffic picks up speed again.

**The turn:** The one who wrecks it is the driver who sees a slow police car as a chance to overtake. They come out of the front at full speed into the gap, which is exactly where someone is standing in a lane. So in California, passing a patrol car that is running a traffic break is a moving violation.

**The ending:** The ladder lies on the shoulder, the patrol car's lights go dark and it slides over to the side, and the held-back cars roll past and spread out across the lanes. Said: it wasn't driving badly. It was making an empty piece of freeway, just long enough to pick up a ladder.

## Where it's known from

California Highway Patrol, Sgt. Brian Pennings, in ABC30's Know the Road with the CHP, 13 Nov 2020: officers 'run what we call a traffic break' for hazards such as objects in the roadway; they activate their lights and 'drive in a serpentine motion back and forth' to slow traffic 'to a safe speed' before it reaches the hazard; passing is illegal, and not staying behind the patrol car 'is a moving violation'. Utah Highway Patrol's own page, Traffic Slow Downs (undated, live when read): the trooper drives slowly 'swerving back and forth (serpentine)' or straddling the lane line with lights on, which 'creates a temporary break in traffic to allow for safety up ahead', used for removing dangerous debris from the road; 'Do not attempt to pass the Trooper': https://uhp.utah.gov/?p=3254 Still done today: the weakest of this batch. The CHP's freeway patrol manual (HPM 70.6, chapter 13) has a traffic breaks section and by a search summary was revised in December 2025, but the PDF could not be read; the Utah page is live but undated; no dated report of a single traffic break from the last two years was found. Reasoned by us, not stated by either patrol: that the gap opens because the cars already ahead keep their speed (the Utah page says only 'a temporary break in traffic'); that the freeway isn't simply stopped; who drags the hazard off (the patrols say only that it is removed); the ladder, the five lanes and 65 mph are our example.

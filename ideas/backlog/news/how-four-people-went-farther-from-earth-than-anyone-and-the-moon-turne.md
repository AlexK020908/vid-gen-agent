- **What's at stake:** Four people a quarter of a million miles from home in a capsule the size of a small van, out of radio contact for about forty minutes behind the Moon, with no quick way to turn back.

## How it goes

**Area:** a record, in space

**Happened:** On 6 April 2026 the Artemis II crew passed the distance record Apollo 13 had held since 1970 and reached 252,756 miles from Earth. They did it by looping once round the far side of the Moon without stopping, and splashed down off San Diego on 10 April.

**When:** Launch 1 April 2026; the record passed at 12:56 p.m. CDT on 6 April 2026; splashdown 8:07 p.m. on 10 April 2026. NASA's release on the record is dated 6 April 2026 and its mission recap came after the landing. Finished and settled.

**Newcomer:** An orbit is a ball thrown so hard it keeps missing the ground. A burn is one long push from the engine, like the throw itself: after it, the ship only coasts. Gravity is a dip in a trampoline: roll a marble past the dip and its path bends round it. A free-return path is a throw aimed so that the Moon's dip bends the ship right round its far side and points it back at Earth, with no second throw needed. Loss of signal is the Moon standing between the ship and Earth the way a hill blocks a phone. A heat shield is the thick sole the capsule lands on when it hits the air.

**Lesson:** The record was set by a path chosen for safety: one throw, and the Moon brings you back even if the engine never lights again. NASA's own figure is 252,756 miles, about 4,100 miles past a record that had stood for 56 years.

**Said by:** NASA says it of its own crew, in its release of 6 April 2026 ('NASA's Artemis II Crew Eclipses Record for Farthest Human Spaceflight'): at 12:56 p.m. CDT the crew was 248,655 miles from Earth, passing Apollo 13's 1970 record, with a farthest point of about 252,756 miles. A crew member said on air, 'as we surpass the furthest distance humans have ever traveled from planet Earth'. https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasas-artemis-ii-crew-eclipses-record-for-farthest-human-spaceflight/ . Nobody is accused of anything. The crew are public figures but the short shows four invented stand-ins and names no one.

1. 1 April: a rocket lifts the capsule into a high loop round Earth, four people inside.
2. The next day the engine gives the big push that throws the capsule out of Earth's loop, aimed at where the Moon will be days later. Then the engine is quiet.
3. For days it coasts, slowing all the way like a ball thrown straight up, Earth shrinking behind it.
4. 6 April: it swings behind the Moon, about 4,067 miles above the surface, and for about forty minutes the Moon blocks every radio signal. At 12:56 p.m. it passes Apollo 13's 248,655 miles, and it peaks at 252,756.
5. Nobody steers it round. The Moon's pull bends the path into a hairpin and the capsule comes out the other side already falling back toward Earth. The throw on day two had the way home built into it.
6. 10 April: it hits the air at nearly 35 times the speed of sound, the heat shield takes it, parachutes open, and it splashes down off San Diego.

**The ending:** The capsule bobbing in the sea under three parachutes, and above it the whole figure-of-eight path drawn in light from Earth round the Moon and back. Said: '252,756 miles. One throw, and the Moon sent them home.'

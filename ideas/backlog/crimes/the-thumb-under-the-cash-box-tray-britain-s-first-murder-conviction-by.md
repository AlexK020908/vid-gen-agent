## How it goes

**Kind:** real

**The break:** The smudge and Alfred Stratton's right thumb, enlarged side by side: one at a time, each place where a ridge stops or splits in two lights up in the same spot on both, about a dozen of them, until it's plainly one thumb.

**Convicted:** Alfred Stratton and Albert Ernest Stratton, of the wilful murder of Thomas Farrow, at the Central Criminal Court (the Old Bailey), May 1905; both hanged at Wandsworth on 23 May 1905. The record's indictment is for Thomas Farrow's murder; accounts say the charge for Ann Farrow's death wasn't proceeded with.

**People:** Thomas and Ann Farrow, the elderly couple who ran the shop (named, as the record names them); the shop boy who finds them (role); the inspector at the fingerprint office, Charles Collins (named); the brothers Alfred and Albert Stratton (named, convicted). Invented stand-ins, no likenesses.

**Shown as:** Told, never shown: the shop's door still shut at opening time, a lamp out upstairs, a soft ring of light where they were found. No bodies, no blood, nothing of the attack.

**The ending:** The two enlarged prints sliding together into a single thumb, beside the little tin tray. Said: 'The milkman couldn't pick them out. The tray could. Both brothers were hanged that May, in what's remembered as Britain's first murder conviction won with a fingerprint.'

## Where it's known from

Old Bailey Proceedings, session of 2 May 1905, trial of Alfred and Albert Ernest Stratton (t19050502-415): the wilful murder of Thomas Farrow, guilty, sentence of death. Inspector Collins's evidence that the mark under the cash box tray was Alfred's right thumb, and that it wasn't the Farrows' or the sergeant's, is in the accounts of the trial, and the cash box is said to be in the Metropolitan Police's Crime Museum. I couldn't open the record's text (the site refuses automated reads); its index shows the indictment, the verdict and the sentence. Accounts differ on how many points matched (11 or 12).

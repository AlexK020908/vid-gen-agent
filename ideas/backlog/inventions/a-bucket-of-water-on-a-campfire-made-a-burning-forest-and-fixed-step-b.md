# A bucket of water on a campfire, made a burning forest and fixed step by step into a water bomber

## The first second

**Heard:** Throw a bucket of water on a campfire and it's out. Now the fire is a whole forest.

**Seen:** A hand tipping a bucket of water onto a small campfire, the flames going out in a burst of steam.

## Why someone would stop

- **With the sound off:** A bucket puts out a campfire, then the picture pulls back to a whole hillside burning above a small town, and the same bucket looks like a toy in front of it. A viewer sees at once that the thing in their hand is useless here.
- **What grabs them:** A simple problem built up to an industrial fix. The everyday thing: throwing a bucket of water on a campfire. The twist: the fire is a forest. It ends on the water bomber that fills itself from a lake without stopping.
- **What's at stake:** The job is to get water onto the fire faster than the fire grows, with a town below it. These planes were over Los Angeles in January 2025: one of Quebec's was working the Palisades Fire on 9 January when a drone struck its wing.
- **The answer you watch:** The bucket's splash vanishing against the fire front; belly doors opening and six tonnes of water falling in one mass; the plane touching a lake at speed, two small scoops under its hull throwing water up into the tanks as a gauge fills in twelve seconds; the spare water spilling out of the overflow ports; the plane lifting off and dropping, again and again.

## How it goes

**Everyday:** Throwing a bucket of water on a campfire to put it out.

**Goal:** Get enough water onto a forest fire, fast enough, to stop it reaching the town.

**Invention:** the water bomber

**Seen it:** On the news every fire season, skimming lakes and reservoirs and dropping on the fire front: over Los Angeles in January 2025, and across Canada, France, Italy, Greece, Spain and Croatia. A new model, the DHC-515, went into assembly in Calgary in March 2026.

**Cycle:** skim, scoop, climb, drop

**Inside:** The plane's belly made glass as it skims: the two scoops under the hull, water rushing up into the four tanks, a level gauge rising, the overflow spilling; then the four doors swinging open over the fire.

1. You can't get near it with a bucket and there's no road for a hose, so the water goes up in a plane and falls on the fire from above, out of four doors in its belly: the tanks and drop doors.
2. One drop and it's empty, with the nearest runway far behind it, so the plane is built as a boat that can set down on any lake near the fire: the flying-boat hull.
3. Stopping on the lake to fill up takes too long, so it never stops: it skims the surface at 130 kilometres an hour with two small scoops down, and its own speed rams the lake up into the tanks, six tonnes in twelve seconds: the scoops.
4. Six tonnes heavier in twelve seconds, the tanks would overfill, so the spare water spills straight out through ports in the side as it climbs away: the overflow ports.
5. Plain water runs off the trees and steams away, so foam concentrate is mixed in as it loads: the foam system.
6. With a lake ten kilometres from the fire, it drops about nine loads before it needs fuel.

**The ending:** The plane skimming the lake, tanks filling in twelve seconds, climbing over the ridge and opening its doors on the fire front above the town, then turning back for the lake: "Congratulations, you just built a water bomber."

## Where it's known from

Wikipedia, Canadair CL-415: it can 'scoop up to 6,140 L' in a 'twelve-second 410-metre-long' run 'at 70 knots (130 km/h; 81 mph)', needing 1,340 m of clear length in all; water can be mixed 'with a chemical foam if desired'; a photo caption shows it 'spilling excess water through underwing overflow ports'; operators in 2026 include Quebec, Ontario, France, Italy, Greece, Spain, Croatia and Bridger Aerospace in the US; assembly of the first DHC-515 began in Calgary in March 2026. Airport Technology's page on the type: 'Two water scoops are installed behind the planing step', 'the water tanks can be filled in 12s by the scoops', four tanks with 'a combined capacity of 6,137l', 'four independently openable water doors', and a mission 'dropping typically nine tank loads of water on a fire, refilling from a water source 10km away'. Wikipedia, Canadair CL-215 (its parent): first flown 23 October 1967, 'the first to be developed from the outset as a water bomber', ordered by Quebec to replace ageing Canso flying boats. In use today: CNN and others, 9 to 10 January 2025, on the Quebec CL-415 struck by a drone over the Palisades Fire.

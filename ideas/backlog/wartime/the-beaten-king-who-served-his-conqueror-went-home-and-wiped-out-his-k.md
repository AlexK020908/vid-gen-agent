# The beaten king who served his conqueror, went home, and wiped out his kingdom 21 years later

- **Kind:** wartime, built as `true_story`
- **Your answer so far:** not shown to you yet

## The first second

**Heard:** King Goujian has 5,000 soldiers left. He offers to be his conqueror's servant.

**Seen:** A map table of China's east coast south of the Yangtze's mouth, stamped 494 BC. In the north, round a big lake, a wide mass of green blocks under a paper tag on a pin: 'FUCHAI, king of Wu'. In the south, on one painted hill marked Kuaiji, a single small yellow block with green blocks closed in a ring round it, under its tag: 'GOUJIAN, king of Yue, 5,000 soldiers left'.

## Why someone would stop

- **With the sound off:** One small block, ringed on a hill by a mass many times its size; then the ring opens and the small block goes and sits beside the mass that beat it. It reads as a surrender, but it is a map table and not a strange sight: the silent test passes only that far.
- **What grabs them:** A king hands himself to the man who has just beaten him, and the conqueror, told to finish the job, lets him go home.
- **What's at stake:** Goujian's life and his whole kingdom, on the day of Kuaiji. Twenty-one years later, Fuchai's.
- **The answer you watch:** The ring closing on one block; the block sliding north and sitting small beside Wu's mass while a counter runs three years; the block going home and new blocks rising round it, one after another, under a counter of years; Wu's mass sliding far north off its own land and leaving a thin line behind; Yue's arrow going into the gap; Wu's green draining away and yellow spreading over it as the year counter stops on 21.

## How it goes

**War:** The wars between two neighbouring kingdoms on China's east coast, Wu and Yue, 494 to 473 BC, at the end of the Spring and Autumn period. Wu, round the big lake south of the Yangtze, was the stronger; Yue, to its south, the weaker.

**Problem:** Stay alive, and keep a kingdom, after losing a war to a neighbour strong enough to finish you.

**How known:** Told by three early books, at different distances from the events, and they don't agree on everything. The Zuozhuan (generally dated to the 4th century BC, a century or more after: that dating is general knowledge, not checked here) has the skeleton, read on Chinese Wikisource: the defeat at Fujiao and the 5,000 on Kuaiji, the peace made through Wu's chief minister, Wu Zixu's warning (Duke Ai, year 1, which is 494 BC), Yue's attack while Fuchai was at Huangchi (year 13, 482 BC) and Wu's end (year 22, 473 BC). It says nothing of Goujian serving in Wu. The Guoyu (Warring States period) adds the service, in two chapters that differ: one says he 'humbly served Fuchai', sent 300 of his men to serve in Wu and 'himself ran before Fuchai's horse'; the other that he went to Wu with his minister Fan Li and was sent home after three years. Sima Qian's Shiji (about 100 BC, nearly four centuries after) has him offer himself and his wife but send two ministers as hostages, and is the first of the three to mention the gall. So 'served his conqueror' is the Guoyu's version, and the short's second line should say 'as the old accounts tell it'. Added later still: 'sleeping on brushwood' is in none of the three (Chinese Wikipedia's article on the idiom, read: the four words 卧薪尝胆 first appear together in a piece by Su Shi, in the 11th century, about a different ruler). The beauty Xi Shi sent to distract Fuchai, and other famous scenes, come from later books I did not open, and the card uses none of them. Wu Zixu's 'twenty years' is too neat to take as a recorded forecast: the book that reports it was written after Wu fell (my own caution, not a historian's I read). The BC years are the standard conversion of the Zuozhuan's reign years, not checked against a table. Both kings were real: weapons with their names cast into them survive. Not opened: Paul Cohen's Speaking to History (2009), on how the story grew, or any modern history of Wu and Yue.

**Left today:** The Sword of Goujian: found in December 1965 in a tomb at Wangshan, Jiangling, Hubei; 55.6 cm long, with 'an almost untarnished blade' that 'still has a sharp blade', and eight characters on it read as 'King of Yue, Goujian, made for his own use' (the two characters of the name were settled after two months of argument among scholars, by letter). It is in the Hubei Provincial Museum in Wuhan (Wikipedia's article on the sword, read). A spear with Fuchai's name on it, found in the same county in 1983, is said to be in the same museum: seen in a search summary only. And the idiom 卧薪尝胆, which Chinese schoolchildren learn (general knowledge).

**Shown as:** Blocks, tags and counters on a map: no figures, no hand. The kings are named on tags and never drawn. Defeat is a ring of blocks closing round one block. The service in Wu is the small block sliding north to sit beside the big mass under a counter of three years. Wu's end is its colour draining from the map and Fuchai's tag lifting away: the record says he took his own life, which is neither shown nor said. The gall is a line of text under Sima Qian's name, not a picture.

**People:** Goujian, king of Yue, the weaker kingdom: beaten in 494 BC, he wanted first to stay alive and keep a kingdom, then to undo the defeat. Fuchai, king of Wu, the winner: he wanted to be first among the rulers of the Chinese states, he took the peace when his chief minister, Bo Pi, spoke for it (bribed by Yue, in Sima Qian's telling), and years later turned north. Wu Zixu, Fuchai's minister, who wanted Yue finished off while it was down, and was overruled. Wen Zhong and Fan Li, Goujian's ministers: Wen Zhong carried the plea for peace, and in Sima Qian's version it was Fan Li who went to Wu as a hostage.

**Lesson:** So when you've lost and can't win yet, think of Goujian: bow, go home, rebuild, and wait for the day they look away. Sayings: the story's own idiom is 卧薪尝胆, 'sleep on brushwood, taste gall'; only the gall is in Sima Qian (about 100 BC), and the brushwood was joined on over a thousand years later. The oldest line is Wu Zixu's warning in the Zuozhuan, 十年生聚，十年教训: 'ten years to grow, ten years to train'. The owner's 先当孙子，后当爷 has this story's shape, but I found no source for where it comes from, and none that ties it to Goujian.

1. 494 BC: Fuchai of Wu beats Yue's army at Fujiao and marches into Yue. Goujian is left holding one mountain, Kuaiji, with 5,000 men in armour. He cannot win and he cannot get away.
2. So he sends to ask for peace on the winner's terms. In Sima Qian's telling the offer is himself: Goujian will be Fuchai's subject, and his wife a maid in Fuchai's house.
3. Fuchai's minister Wu Zixu tells him to finish Yue now: give it ten years to grow and ten to train, and 'beyond twenty years, Wu will be a pond'. Fuchai takes the peace.
4. As the Guoyu tells it, Goujian then serves in Wu for three years, running in front of Fuchai's horse, until Wu sends him home. (Sima Qian has him send two ministers as hostages instead.)
5. At home he rebuilds: the Guoyu lists a reward for every birth (two jugs of wine and a dog for a boy, two jugs and a pig for a girl) and ten years with no taxes. Sima Qian adds that he kept a gall bladder by his seat and tasted it, asking himself: 'Have you forgotten the shame of Kuaiji?'
6. 482 BC: Fuchai takes his best troops far north, to a meeting of the rulers at Huangchi. Yue strikes Wu behind his back. In 473 BC, after a siege that the Guoyu and Sima Qian both put at three years, Wu is destroyed: twenty-one years after Kuaiji.

**The ending:** The table in 473 BC: Wu's green has drained off the map and Yue's yellow lies over both kingdoms, a year counter stopped on 21, Fuchai's tag gone. Then the real thing: a bronze sword in a museum case, the words on its blade lit: 'King of Yue, made for his own use'. Said: 'So when you've lost and can't win yet, think of Goujian: bow, go home, rebuild, and wait for the day they look away. His sword was dug up in 1965. It was still sharp.'
